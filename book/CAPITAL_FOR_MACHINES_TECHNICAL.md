# CAPITAL FOR MACHINES

## On the Production, Accumulation, and Correction of Reasons

---

# PART I — THE ANSWER

## 1. The Answer

A machine produces an answer.

We look at the answer and ask whether it is right.

This is already too late.

Before the answer appeared, a world had to be prepared for it. Something had to count as an object. Something else had to count as evidence. A representation had to make some paths cheap and other paths difficult. A transformation had to carry material across that representation. At the end of the process, a sentence appeared on a screen.

We are good at looking at the sentence.

It is the part we can see.

Suppose it is right. We use it. We send it to someone else. We place it in a report. We ask the machine another question and allow the answer to become part of the prompt. We build an action around it.

The answer has not changed.

What has changed is what the answer can do.

At first it was an output. Then it became a premise. It entered the machinery that would produce the next answer.

That is the first transformation this book asks you to notice.

A conclusion is not merely a thing.

**A conclusion is something that happened.**

Something was observed or supplied. Some distinctions were available and others were not. The material was represented. A transformation operated over it. Alternatives disappeared. A result survived.

The visible answer is the surviving surface of a process that has mostly vanished.

Usually, this is a gift.

No one wants to reconstruct the whole history of arithmetic every time a calculator displays a number. A system that had to rediscover every useful distinction before acting would never get anywhere. Intelligence requires carrying the past forward in a form that can be used without paying the full cost of its discovery again.

But what is carried forward is smaller than what produced it.

And what is left out may be exactly what a future contradiction needs.

## 2. Trust

The answer arrives before its history does.

That order makes trust easy.

The result is clean. The route is not. The machine does not show every discarded alternative, every boundary it accepted, every condition under which its conclusion was earned. It gives us the part that can be acted on.

We act.

The answer works once. It works again. Each successful use makes the absent history feel less important. The machine has not proved that its route will remain sound. It has shown that the route has not yet failed.

This is how a useful answer becomes a shortcut.

The shortcut is not a lie. It is a compressed history.

Compression saves time. It also distributes the future unevenly. Some questions become cheap because the shortcut was built to answer them. Other questions become expensive because the distinctions needed to ask them were compressed away.

The question is not simply what the machine forgot.

The question is what a future contradiction would need to recover.

# PART II — THE USE

## 3. The Map

Imagine two teams given the same question, the same evidence, and one afternoon in which to answer it.

The first team receives a table. Every event is a row. Every row has the same shape. The second receives a map. Events that are far apart in the table appear adjacent on the map because they share a relation the table does not display.

The first team searches rows.

The second team notices a path.

Neither team was given more reality. One team was given a representation in which the path was cheap. For the other, it was expensive enough to remain unseen.

Representation is often treated as a passive mirror. It is not. It changes what can be seen, compared, stored, and reached under a finite budget.

Only now does the notation become useful.

Let $\Omega$ be a space of tasks or states, $A$ a bounded resource budget, and $C_B(x)$ the cost of reaching $x$ in a baseline environment $B$. The reachable set is:

$$
R(B)=\{x\in\Omega\mid C_B(x)\leq A\}.
$$

A different representation can enlarge that set. It can also hide the distinctions that would tell us the set was wrong.

The map does not merely show the future.

It changes which futures are available at the cost of thought.

When a system chooses what counts as an object, it constrains what can later count as a relation. When it constrains the relations that can be expressed, it constrains what can later be identified as a cause. And that constrains what can later be corrected.

The world arrives already translated.

That translation can be faithful, useful, and incomplete at the same time.

## 4. The Route

A conclusion is not simply a node in a graph. It is the product of an operation.

$$
K_1,K_2\xrightarrow{\tau}K_3.
$$

The operation may combine observations, apply a rule, select a candidate, compress a trace, update authority, or alter the mechanism that will generate later updates.

The result does not contain the whole route that produced it.

That matters the first time a route is challenged.

A system may preserve where a statement came from while losing the rule that allowed it to function as a premise. It may preserve the evidence while losing the composition that gave the evidence its force.

The truth of a component is not the truth of the composition.

An accurate prediction can come from a route that was not entitled to carry the meaning later assigned to it. The answer can be right while the path by which it acquired authority is wrong.

For a while, this distinction has no visible consequence.

Then the route is reused.

# PART III — THE INHERITANCE

## 5. The Second Life

The first time a conclusion appears, it may be an answer.

The second time it becomes a premise, it becomes productive.

Past reasoning has become a condition of future reasoning.

A reason becomes capital when future reasoning can put it to work without paying its original cost of discovery again.

This is not a metaphor about money. It is a description of a change in function.

A reason in an inaccessible archive is history. A reason that can enter a later transformation is productive. Accumulated reasoning structure with productive access to the future is reasoning capital.

The reason now has a second life.

It no longer merely tells the machine something. It helps determine what the machine can tell itself next.

The question is no longer only whether the answer is good.

What does this piece of reasoning make possible afterward?

An answer can become a method. A method can become a filter. A filter can become an evaluator. A representation can change the search space in which the next representation is found.

Reason can become a means of producing more reason.

## 6. What Survives

When a system is passed forward, it rarely receives the whole history of its construction.

It receives a body, a parameter, a file, a rule, a label, an interface, a score. It receives the result in a form that can be used by something that did not witness its production.

The machine may preserve:

$$
K_5
$$

while losing:

$$
K_1
\rightarrow
\tau_1
\rightarrow
K_2
\rightarrow
\tau_2
\rightarrow
K_3
\rightarrow
\tau_3
\rightarrow
K_4
\rightarrow
\tau_4
\rightarrow
K_5.
$$

The final structure survives. The conditions that gave it meaning, scope, and authority do not necessarily survive with it.

Inheritance is how the future avoids paying the full cost of the past.

It is also how the future can receive more authority than the past earned.

The recurring danger is not ordinary error. It is unearned inheritance: a reason crossing a boundary with more authority than it earned locally.

## 7. The Construction

At first, a distinction is a decision.

Someone—or some process—separates two cases because a prediction, a consequence, or a practical need makes the separation useful.

Then the distinction is reused.

Models are built on it. Policies rely on it. Evaluations assume it. Later systems inherit representations in which it is already encoded.

Eventually nobody asks whether the distinction should exist.

The construction remains.

The conditions that made it contingent disappear.

What was done begins to look like what is.

That is naturalization.

The machine remembers how things are and forgets that this was once something the system did.

The forgotten action may be where authority entered.

# PART IV — THE CRACK

## 8. The Coin

Anton Chigurh flips a coin.

The coin is real. The toss is real. The result is accurately reported.

But the frame was made by the man.

He chooses the coin. He chooses that the toss will decide. He chooses what the two sides will mean. He chooses the moment at which the result will be presented as binding.

The coin can land exactly as it lands.

The history can be accurately recited.

The authority is still manufactured.

There is no need to falsify an event in order to turn it into a mandate.

The frame does that work.

## 9. The Defeater

Eventually something happens that should not have happened.

A prediction fails. A policy produces the opposite of its intended consequence. Two successful lines of reasoning collide. Reality refuses to cooperate.

Now there are descendants.

One has independent support. One has no support beyond the defeated route. A third was computed using the first but did not inherit its warrant.

The diagram is small enough to fit on a page:

The first descendant may survive. The second may not. The third may have to be reopened even if its result remains true.

The transformation is defeated.

What do you delete?

If you delete everything, you confuse historical dependence with present warrant. If you delete nothing, you confuse surviving truth with surviving authority.

Reality has told you that something failed.

It has not told you which descendants must disappear.

The question is not whether the final statement was true.

Which authority entered through the thing reality just defeated?

# PART V — THE REOPENING

## 10. Surgery

The naive response to a defeated reason is deletion.

```text
defeated claim → delete claim → continue
```

But deletion destroys history and can remove independently warranted descendants.

Correction is surgery.

Find the defeated warrant. Trace what received authority from it. Preserve what has another support. Reopen what depended on the route. Record what changed and what did not.

A reason can survive the loss of one history if another independent history still supports it. Correction needs a map of which reasons support which descendants, and which routes remain independent when one fails.

Several weaker paths may compose into stronger warrant, but multiplicity is not independence. The routes must remain visible, compatible in scope, and governed by an explicit rule.

The transformation happened.

Its history remains.

Its authority does not necessarily remain.

## 11. The Sealed Machine

A machine can rewrite itself endlessly.

It can produce new rules, new representations, new prompts, new policies, and new evaluators.

And still remain sealed from correction.

Every change may be generated from inside the same closure. The system can move its outputs while the world remains unable to reach the machinery that decides what kinds of movement are permitted.

Self-modification is not the same as open-ended evolution.

The difficult question is not whether reality can change an output. It is whether reality can reach the mechanism that determines how future outputs will be changed.

Ordinary adaptation looks like this:

```text
reality → state / knowledge / behavior
```

The deeper route is:

```text
reality → revision mechanism → future adaptive mechanism space
```

Changing a representation does not necessarily change the generator that will produce the next one.

The system must be open enough to change and bounded enough not to be changed by every noise source.

## 12. The One-Way Door

A thing can be stored without being recoverable.

It can be recoverable without being triggerable.

It can be triggerable without being actionable.

A system may contain the original text but lack the index needed to retrieve it. It may retrieve the text but lack the condition that tells it to reopen the issue. It may recognize the condition but lack permission or mechanism to act on the recognition.

This is how history becomes unreachable without being destroyed.

A summary can restore conclusions while omitting their unresolved status. A checkpoint can restore parameters while omitting the authority relations that made those parameters operative. A benchmark can restore a score while omitting the contamination that should have prevented promotion.

Restoration is not conclusion-only recall.

It is recovery of the state in which conclusions, uncertainties, provenance, pending corrections, and authorization were related.

# PART VI — THE WRONG OBJECT

## 13. The Innocent Variables

Suppose every local inference is correct.

Suppose every observable property has been measured accurately.

Suppose the mechanism operates exactly as specified.

Suppose the machine nevertheless fails.

The machine searches for the bad variable.

None.

It searches for the bad datum.

None.

It searches for the bad mechanism.

None.

Everything it knows how to inspect is innocent.

There was no defective object in the set it was inspecting.

The set itself was the mistake.

The machine may be reasoning correctly about the wrong objects.

## 14. Of What?

Perhaps the machine sees:

$$
o_1,\quad o_2,\quad o_3,\quad o_4
$$

and there is nothing wrong with what it believes about any of them.

But the consequential phenomenon exists only in:

$$
o_1\rightarrow o_2\rightarrow o_3,
$$

or in the set they form together, or in a relation crossing objects the inherited representation treats as separate.

Every object the machine knows how to inspect may appear innocent.

Resolution asks: **How finely?**

Unitization asks: **Of what?**

A microscope gives finer detail about the object under inspection. It does not tell you that the relevant object was the interaction between three things placed on separate slides.

More information does not guarantee a better object.

More accurate reasoning does not guarantee a better question.

The alternative object cannot be authorized merely by being imaginable. Failure does not tell you the replacement. A candidate must be proposed, discriminated, and tested.

The problem has moved beneath the answer.

What if the machine's categories prevent it from noticing what kind of mistake it is making?

# PART VII — THE TEST

## 15. The Benchmark

An evaluator is never merely outside the system it measures.

It decides what counts as an answer, what counts as failure, what information is visible, which distinctions exist, and what can be discovered.

If the answer is exposed, the test measures retrieval or compliance rather than discovery.

If the answer is made inaccessible, the test measures the designer's secrecy rather than the learner's capacity.

The evaluator is part of the world it claims to measure.

That is why a successful prediction is not necessarily earned evidence.

The answer can be right.

The test can still fail.

## 16. The Result We Threw Away

We once obtained exactly the result we had hoped to obtain.

The output was right.

For a moment, the experiment appeared to have crossed the boundary we were trying to test.

Then we audited how the answer had become possible.

The setup had allowed the answer family and the constructor to meet in ways that the experiment was supposed to keep independent. The result could remain an interesting observation. It could not carry the interpretation we wanted to give it.

So we threw the result away.

Not the bytes. Not the history. **The interpretation.**

The machine got the answer right.

The prediction was exact.

The experiment looked successful.

And the result was rejected.

The observation survived. The stronger interpretation did not.

This is how a research process becomes more intelligent without becoming more convinced of itself.

## 17. The Reader

By now, the reader has been handed a sequence of distinctions.

An answer and the event that produced it.

A shortcut and the history compressed inside it.

A useful reason and the infrastructure it can become.

An inherited structure and the authority it may carry across a boundary.

A defeated route and the descendants that do not all deserve the same fate.

An object and the possibility that the object was wrong.

The sequence is also a test.

The reader can accept the vocabulary without accepting the argument. The reader can understand a claim, reconstruct its route, identify its scope, and still reject it.

That is not a failure of the book.

It is the condition under which the book remains revisable.

At some point the reader notices that the text has supplied its own representation. It has chosen a sequence. It has made certain distinctions easy to see and others difficult to see. It has offered examples, withheld others, and decided when an equation should arrive.

The reader has been inside an evaluator.

The book has been accumulating authority while asking not to be granted authority for free.

The question is no longer only whether the machine can revise its reasons.

It is whether the reader can still revise the reasons through which the reader has been taught to read.

# EPILOGUE — WHAT REMAINS

A machine produces an answer.

The answer becomes useful.

Useful answers become premises.

Premises become methods.

Methods become infrastructure.

Infrastructure becomes invisible.

Then something happens that the infrastructure cannot explain.

The machine looks for the error.

The question is whether it can still see the thing it needs to change.
