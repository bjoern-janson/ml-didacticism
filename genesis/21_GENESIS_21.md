# Genesis 21 — Legibility-First Structural Decoding

**Source:** pinned King James Version (KJV) corpus  
**Purpose:** make the chapter legible before interpretation  
**Status:** working structural reading; categories may evolve when later text requires them

The governing boundary remains:

```math
\boxed{\text{TEXT} \neq \text{STRUCTURAL PARSE} \neq \text{INTERPRETATION}}
```

and the working question for this chapter is:

```math
\boxed{\textbf{How does Genesis 21 represent a previously stated future becoming a present state, and what new relations follow from that change?}}
```

Genesis 17 names Isaac in advance, assigns him a Sarah-specific future, and gives a set-time marker. Genesis 18 repeats a Sarah-son future statement. Genesis 21 opens by explicitly connecting those prior statements to a narrated birth. The chapter then moves through naming and circumcision, growth and weaning, Sarah's observation of Hagar's son, a demanded household separation, Abraham's distress, God's descendant-specific response, Hagar and the lad's departure, resource depletion and recovery, Ishmael's continuing trajectory, and finally a distinct Abraham–Abimelech oath/covenant and well-dispute sequence.

A first-pass chapter grammar is therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{prior future statement}
\rightarrow
\text{realized birth}
\rightarrow
\text{naming + circumcision}
\rightarrow
\text{growth / weaning / feast}
\rightarrow
\text{observation}
\rightarrow
\text{household separation demand}
\rightarrow
\text{Abraham distress}
\rightarrow
\text{divine instruction + descendant differentiation}
\rightarrow
\text{departure with provisions}
\rightarrow
\text{resource depletion / distress}
\rightarrow
\text{heard cry + instruction}
\rightarrow
\text{well observation / water recovery}
\rightarrow
\text{Ishmael independent trajectory}
\;||\;
\text{Abimelech recognition / oath proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{well dispute + information correction}
\rightarrow
\text{covenant / witness transfer / place naming}
}
```

The main discipline for this chapter is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{future statement}
\neq
\text{realized event}
\neq
\text{birth}
\neq
\text{naming}
\neq
\text{circumcision}
\neq
\text{household status}
\neq
\text{separation}
\neq
\text{future population branch}
}
```

# 1. Genesis 21:1–2 — Prior speech becomes narrated birth

## Plain rendering

The LORD visits Sarah as he had said and does to Sarah as he had spoken. Sarah conceives and bears Abraham a son in his old age at the set time of which God had spoken to him.

## Explicit correspondence language

The chapter itself supplies three backward links:

```text
LORD → visits Sarah → as he had said
LORD → does unto Sarah → as he had spoken
Sarah → bears son → at set time God had spoken
```

This is stronger than a reader-imposed correspondence.

```math
\boxed{
\text{prior future statement}
\rightarrow
\text{later narrated event correspondence}
}
```

while preserving:

```math
\boxed{
\text{prior statement}
\neq
\text{later event}
}
```

## Cross-chapter chain

A cautious local chain is:

```text
Genesis 17 → Sarah will bear Isaac at a set time in the next year
Genesis 18 → Sarah will have a son according to the time of life
Genesis 21 → Sarah conceives and bears a son at the set time spoken
```

Genesis 21 itself explicitly identifies the event as corresponding to prior speech. We do not need a general theory of prophecy to preserve that relation.

# 2. Genesis 21:3–5 — Birth, naming, circumcision, age marker

## Plain rendering

Abraham names the son Sarah bears Isaac. Abraham circumcises Isaac at eight days old as God had commanded. Abraham is one hundred years old when Isaac is born.

## Distinct operations

```text
Sarah → bears son to Abraham
Abraham → names son Isaac
Isaac → circumcised at 8 days
Abraham → performs circumcision
reason/correspondence → as God commanded
Abraham → age 100 at Isaac's birth
```

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{birth}
\neq
\text{name assignment}
\neq
\text{covenant-token implementation}
\neq
\text{age indexing}
}
```

Genesis 17 had instructed that the son be called Isaac and that eight-day-old males be circumcised. Genesis 21 now narrates both corresponding actions.

# 3. Genesis 21:6–8 — Sarah's interpretation, child growth, weaning, feast

## Plain rendering

Sarah says God has made her to laugh and that those who hear will laugh with her. She remarks on bearing Abraham a son in his old age. Isaac grows and is weaned, and Abraham makes a great feast on the day Isaac is weaned.

## Sarah's speech

```text
Sarah → attributes laughter state → God made me to laugh
Sarah → represents future/social response → all who hear will laugh with me
Sarah → states surprising prior expectation → who would have said ... ?
Sarah → states present result → borne Abraham a son in old age
```

These are Sarah's stated interpretations and representations.

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{narrated birth}
\neq
\text{Sarah's interpretation of the birth}
}
```

## Maturation sequence

```text
Isaac → grows
Isaac → weaned
same day → Abraham makes great feast
```

Do not infer Isaac's exact age at weaning; Genesis 21 does not supply it.

# 4. Genesis 21:9–11 — Observation, Sarah's demand, heir relation, Abraham's distress

## Plain rendering

Sarah sees the son of Hagar the Egyptian, whom Hagar bore to Abraham, `mocking`. Sarah tells Abraham to cast out the bondwoman and her son, stating that the bondwoman's son will not be heir with Isaac. The matter is very grievous in Abraham's sight because of his son.

## Observation

```text
Sarah → sees → Hagar's son
Hagar's son → described as mocking
```

The structural layer should preserve the KJV term `mocking` without defining a more specific act unless later text requires it.

## Sarah's requested action

```text
Sarah → tells Abraham → cast out bondwoman
Sarah → tells Abraham → cast out her son
```

## Sarah's stated reason / future relation

```text
Hagar's son → shall not be heir with Isaac
```

This is Sarah's explicit heir relation statement.

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Sarah's observation}
\neq
\text{Sarah's demanded action}
\neq
\text{Sarah's heir statement}
}
```

The chapter places them in sequence, but a complete psychological/causal explanation of Sarah's demand should not be invented beyond her stated words.

## Abraham's reaction

```text
matter → very grievous in Abraham's sight
explicit reason → because of his son
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Sarah's proposed separation}
\neq
\text{Abraham's emotional/evaluative response}
}
```

# 5. Genesis 21:12–13 — Instruction to Abraham and differentiated descendant futures

## Plain rendering

God tells Abraham not to let the matter be grievous because of the lad and the bondwoman and tells him to hearken to Sarah. The reason given is that Abraham's seed will be called in Isaac. God also says he will make a nation from the bondwoman's son because that son is Abraham's seed.

## Instruction

```text
God → tells Abraham → do not let matter be grievous
God → tells Abraham → hearken to Sarah's voice
```

## Isaac relation

```text
in Isaac → Abraham's seed shall be called
```

## Ishmael relation

```text
bondwoman's son → God will make nation
explicit reason → because he is Abraham's seed
```

The chapter therefore preserves differentiated futures:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Isaac-specific seed-calling relation}
\neq
\text{Ishmael nation-future relation}
}
```

and also:

```math
\boxed{
\text{household separation}
\neq
\text{destruction / cancellation of Ishmael's future}
}
```

This continues Genesis 17's distinction between Isaac-specific covenant language and Ishmael-specific blessing/nation language.

# 6. Genesis 21:14 — Provision, departure, wilderness state

## Plain rendering

Abraham rises early, gives Hagar bread and a bottle of water, places them on her shoulder along with the child, sends her away, and Hagar departs and wanders in the wilderness of Beersheba.

## Departure sequence

```text
Abraham → rises early
Abraham → supplies bread
Abraham → supplies bottle of water
Abraham → gives provisions to Hagar
Abraham → sends Hagar away
Hagar → departs
Hagar → wanders in wilderness of Beersheba
```

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{separation instruction}
\neq
\text{provision}
\neq
\text{departure}
\neq
\text{later wilderness condition}
}
```

The chapter does not specify a destination chosen by Hagar before saying she wanders in the wilderness.

# 7. Genesis 21:15–19 — Resource depletion, distress, heard cry, instruction, well observation, recovery

## Plain rendering

The water is exhausted. Hagar places the child under a shrub, moves a bowshot away because she does not want to see the child's death, and weeps. God hears the voice of the lad. The angel of God calls to Hagar from heaven, tells her not to fear because God has heard the lad, instructs her to lift and hold him, and repeats the future great-nation statement. God opens Hagar's eyes; she sees a well, fills the bottle, and gives the lad water.

## Resource-state transition

```text
water in bottle → spent
```

This is an explicit depletion state.

## Hagar's represented future / distancing action

```text
Hagar → places child under shrub
Hagar → moves away → about bowshot
Hagar → says → let me not see death of child
Hagar → weeps
```

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Hagar's represented feared outcome}
\neq
\text{narrated realized outcome}
}
```

The child does not die in the chapter.

## Hearing and call

```text
God → hears voice of lad
angel of God → calls Hagar from heaven
angel → asks what troubles Hagar
angel → says fear not
explicit reason → God heard voice of lad where he is
```

Do not turn `God heard` into an unstated generic mechanism. Preserve the explicit relation.

## Instruction + future statement

```text
Hagar → arise
Hagar → lift lad
Hagar → hold him in hand
future → great nation
```

## Observation / resource recovery

```text
God → opens Hagar's eyes
Hagar → sees well of water
Hagar → goes to well
Hagar → fills bottle
Hagar → gives lad drink
```

This gives a strong sequence:

```math
\boxed{
\text{resource depleted}
\rightarrow
\text{distress}
\rightarrow
\text{heard cry / call}
\rightarrow
\text{instruction}
\rightarrow
\text{changed observation state}
\rightarrow
\text{well seen}
\rightarrow
\text{water obtained}
}
```

A crucial restraint:

```math
\boxed{
\text{God opened her eyes}
\rightarrow
\text{she saw a well}
}
```

is explicit, while the chapter does not specify whether the well newly appeared, had previously been visually unavailable, had been unnoticed, or some other mechanism.

That mechanism remains OPEN.

# 8. Genesis 21:20–21 — Ishmael's independent trajectory

## Plain rendering

God is with the lad. He grows, dwells in the wilderness, becomes an archer, dwells in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother obtains a wife for him from Egypt.

## Trajectory

```text
God → with lad
lad/Ishmael → grows
Ishmael → dwells in wilderness
Ishmael → becomes archer
Ishmael → dwells in wilderness of Paran
Hagar → gets wife for Ishmael
wife → from Egypt
```

The chapter therefore moves Ishmael from:

```text
member of Abraham's household
→ separated child with Hagar
→ wilderness survival
→ growing independent male
→ marriage relation
```

This is a household/lineage transition, not extinction.

```math
\boxed{
\text{separation from Abraham's household}
\neq
\text{termination of Ishmael's lineage trajectory}
}
```

# 9. Genesis 21:22–24 — Abimelech recognition and oath proposal

## Plain rendering

At that time Abimelech and Phichol, chief captain of his host, tell Abraham that God is with him in all he does. Abimelech asks Abraham to swear by God not to deal falsely with him, his son, or his son's son, but to reciprocate the kindness Abimelech has shown him and the land in which Abraham has sojourned. Abraham says he will swear.

## Recognition statement

```text
Abimelech + Phichol → say to Abraham
God → is with Abraham in all Abraham does
```

This is their stated recognition.

## Proposed persistent relation

```text
Abimelech → asks Abraham → swear by God
scope → Abimelech
scope → Abimelech's son
scope → Abimelech's son's son
constraint → do not deal falsely
positive relation → reciprocate kindness
land relation → land wherein Abraham sojourned
```

## Abraham's response

```text
Abraham → says → I will swear
```

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{recognition statement}
\neq
\text{oath proposal}
\neq
\text{Abraham's assent}
\neq
\text{later covenant act}
}
```

# 10. Genesis 21:25–26 — Well dispute and explicit information gap

## Plain rendering

Abraham reproves Abimelech because Abimelech's servants had violently taken away a well. Abimelech says he does not know who did it, that Abraham had not told him, and that he had not heard of it until that day.

## Narrated event / attribution

```text
well of water → violently taken away
agents named by Abraham/narration → Abimelech's servants
Abraham → reproves Abimelech because of well
```

## Abimelech's information state

Abimelech explicitly states:

```text
who did it → not known by Abimelech
Abraham → had not told Abimelech
Abimelech → had not heard of it before today
```

This is one of the chapter's strongest information-provenance edges:

```math
\boxed{
\text{event occurred}
\neq
\text{Abimelech knew event/agent relation}
\neq
\text{Abraham had previously transmitted complaint}
}
```

And the text positively supplies the missing-transmission statement:

```math
\boxed{
\text{prior Abraham→Abimelech complaint transmission}
=
\text{explicitly negated}
}
```

This extends the Genesis 12–20 information-state thread in a particularly clean way.

# 11. Genesis 21:27–32 — Covenant, seven ewe lambs, witness relation, Beersheba naming

## Plain rendering

Abraham gives sheep and oxen to Abimelech, and the two make a covenant. Abraham sets seven ewe lambs apart. Abimelech asks what they mean. Abraham says Abimelech should take them from his hand as a witness that Abraham dug the well. The place is called Beersheba because the two swore there. The chapter repeats that they made a covenant at Beersheba; Abimelech and Phichol then return to the land of the Philistines.

## Transfer + covenant

```text
Abraham → gives sheep + oxen → Abimelech
Abraham + Abimelech → make covenant
```

## Separate witness-object sequence

```text
Abraham → sets seven ewe lambs apart
Abimelech → asks meaning
Abraham → instructs Abimelech to take seven lambs
seven lambs → witness relation
witness content → Abraham dug well
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{general livestock transfer}
\neq
\text{seven-lamb witness transfer}
\neq
\text{covenant itself}
}
```

## Place naming

```text
place → called Beersheba
explicit reason → there they swore both of them
```

The chapter then repeats:

```text
Abraham + Abimelech → made covenant at Beersheba
Abimelech + Phichol → return to Philistines' land
```

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{oath}
\neq
\text{well witness}
\neq
\text{place naming}
\neq
\text{covenant relation}
}
```

although the text directly connects them.

# 12. Genesis 21:33–34 — Abraham's continuing local state

## Plain rendering

Abraham plants a grove in Beersheba, calls there on the name of the LORD, the everlasting God, and sojourns in the Philistines' land many days.

```text
Abraham → plants grove → Beersheba
Abraham → calls on name of LORD
LORD → described as everlasting God
Abraham → sojourns in Philistines' land
quantity → many days
```

The chapter therefore ends not with the well dispute itself, but with a continuing dwelling/sojourning state.

# 13. Genesis 17 → 18 → 21 — A future statement becomes a present child

A clean cross-chapter record is now possible:

```text
Genesis 17
Sarah → will bear son
son → Isaac
set time → next year

Genesis 18
Sarah → will have son
speaker → return according to time of life

Genesis 21
Sarah → conceives
Sarah → bears son
when → set time previously spoken
Abraham → names him Isaac
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{future representation}
\rightarrow
\text{explicitly corresponding narrated realization}
}
```

This is a local textual relation. It does not require converting the project into a prediction benchmark or a general metaphysical theory.

# 14. Isaac and Ishmael are differentiated without flattening either branch

Genesis 21 gives several distinct relations:

```text
Isaac → son born to Sarah
Isaac → named
Isaac → circumcised at 8 days
Isaac → grows / weaned
Isaac → seed-calling relation stated by God

Ishmael / bondwoman's son → separated from Abraham's household
Ishmael → still Abraham's seed
Ishmael → future nation statement
Ishmael → preserved through wilderness crisis
Ishmael → grows / becomes archer / dwells in Paran
Ishmael → receives wife through Hagar
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Isaac's realized + descendant-specific state}
\neq
\text{Ishmael's separated + continuing lineage state}
}
```

and:

```math
\boxed{
\text{household differentiation}
\neq
\text{one branch becoming structurally nonexistent}
}
```

# 15. Information-state thread through Genesis 21

Genesis 21 adds at least two useful information patterns.

## Hagar's observation changes

```text
water depleted
→ distress
→ God hears lad
→ angel speaks
→ God opens Hagar's eyes
→ Hagar sees well
→ Hagar obtains water
```

Keep:

```math
\boxed{
\text{resource existence/state}
\neq
\text{agent observation of resource}
}
```

The text supplies the observation transition but not the underlying visual/physical mechanism.

## Abimelech's explicit prior ignorance

```text
well taken by Abimelech's servants
→ Abraham raises issue
→ Abimelech says he did not know who did it
→ Abraham had not told him
→ Abimelech had not heard before that day
```

So the corpus distinction becomes even sharper:

```math
\boxed{
\text{world event}
\neq
\text{information possessed by A}
\neq
\text{information transmitted by B to A}
}
```

# 17. Chapter compression

Genesis 21 is best represented as two adjacent grammars rather than one flattened chain.

## Household / lineage grammar

```math
\boxed{
\text{promised future realized}
\rightarrow
\text{birth / naming / circumcision}
\rightarrow
\text{growth / feast}
\rightarrow
\text{household differentiation}
\rightarrow
\text{separation}
\rightarrow
\text{resource crisis}
\rightarrow
\text{preservation / recovery}
\rightarrow
\text{independent lineage trajectory}
}
```

## Abraham–Abimelech grammar

```math
\boxed{
\text{recognition}
\rightarrow
\text{oath proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{assent}
\rightarrow
\text{well dispute}
\rightarrow
\text{explicit information correction}
\rightarrow
\text{covenant / witness transfer}
\rightarrow
\text{place naming}
\rightarrow
\text{continued sojourn}
}
```

The strongest chapter-level takeaway is:

```math
\boxed{
\textbf{Genesis 21 turns several represented futures into present states, then shows those realized states generating new household, resource, lineage, and negotiated social relations.}
}
```
