# Genesis 37 — Legibility-First Structural Decoding

**Source:** pinned King James Version (KJV) corpus  
**Purpose:** make the chapter legible before interpretation  
**Status:** working structural reading; categories may evolve when later text requires them

The governing boundary remains:

```math
\boxed{\text{TEXT} \neq \text{STRUCTURAL PARSE} \neq \text{INTERPRETATION}}
```

and the working question for this chapter is:

```math
\boxed{\textbf{How does Genesis 37 transform a real event into different information states for different people?}}
```

Genesis 37 returns sharply from Genesis 36's population-and-authority database grammar to a dense event-and-information grammar. The chapter begins with Joseph embedded in a differentiated household state: he is seventeen, works with his brothers, brings an evil report to his father, is loved by Israel more than the other children, and receives a coat of many colours. The brothers see the preference and hate Joseph. Joseph then dreams, reports the dreams, and different recipients produce different interpretations and reactions.

The chapter later changes from household representation to physical search and concealed action. Israel sends Joseph to check on his brothers and flocks and bring back word. Joseph receives third-party location information and finds his brothers at Dothan. Before Joseph reaches them, the brothers see him and form a plan to kill him and report that an evil beast devoured him. Reuben interrupts that plan and proposes the pit while the narrator separately discloses Reuben's intent to return Joseph to his father. Joseph arrives, is stripped of the coat, and is put into the empty pit. Judah then proposes sale rather than killing. The text introduces Midianite merchantmen and then narrates Joseph being drawn from the pit, sold to Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver, and taken toward Egypt. Reuben later returns to the pit, discovers Joseph absent, and reacts without having that state already available to him.

The coat then becomes an evidence-bearing object. A kid is killed; the coat is dipped in its blood; the coat is sent/brought to Jacob with the statement, `This have we found: know now whether it be thy son's coat or no.` The speakers do not directly utter `Joseph is dead.` Jacob recognizes the coat and supplies the death interpretation himself: an evil beast has devoured Joseph; Joseph is rent in pieces. Jacob's conclusion is false relative to the reader's world-state information, but the resulting grief is real and persistent within the chapter. The chapter closes by preserving both states at once: Jacob mourns a son he believes dead, while Joseph is alive and is sold in Egypt to Potiphar.

A first-pass chapter grammar is therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{household differentiation}
\rightarrow
\text{Joseph report}
\rightarrow
\text{preference + visible status object}
\rightarrow
\text{brotherly hatred}
\rightarrow
\text{dream}
\rightarrow
\text{dream report}
\rightarrow
\text{recipient interpretation / reaction}
\rightarrow
\text{delegated search mission}
\rightarrow
\text{third-party location update}
\rightarrow
\text{brothers see Joseph before he reaches them}
\rightarrow
\text{concealed kill+cover-story plan}
\rightarrow
\text{Reuben intervention + hidden rescue intent}
\rightarrow
\text{coat removal + pit confinement}
\rightarrow
\text{Judah sale proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{physical extraction / sale / transport}
\rightarrow
\text{Reuben non-knowledge + discovery}
\rightarrow
\text{coat/blood manipulation}
\rightarrow
\text{evidence presentation}
\rightarrow
\text{Jacob recognition + inference}
\rightarrow
\text{persistent grief under false belief}
\parallel
\text{Joseph alive in Egypt}
}
```

The main discipline for this chapter is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{world event}
\neq
\text{evidence available to an agent}
\neq
\text{agent inference from evidence}
}
```

A second discipline is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{dream content}
\neq
\text{dream report}
\neq
\text{recipient interpretation}
\neq
\text{later event realization}
}
```

A third is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{proposed action}
\neq
\text{intervening proposal}
\neq
\text{performed action}
}
```

A fourth is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{evidence object}
\neq
\text{evidence provenance}
\neq
\text{conclusion drawn from evidence}
}
```

And the chapter's strongest information principle is:

```math
\boxed{
\textbf{A false conclusion can be induced without directly stating the false proposition.}
}
```

# 1. Genesis 37:1–4 — Joseph's household status, report, unequal love, coat, and brotherly response

## Plain rendering

Jacob dwells in Canaan, the land in which his father had been a stranger. Joseph is seventeen and feeds the flock with his brothers, being with the sons of Bilhah and Zilpah. Joseph brings to his father their evil report. Israel loves Joseph more than all his children because Joseph is the son of his old age and makes him a coat of many colours. Joseph's brothers see that their father loves Joseph more than all his brothers; they hate Joseph and cannot speak peaceably to him.

## Genesis 36 → Genesis 37 grammar change

Genesis 36 ended in compressed population topology:

```text
Esau/Edom
→ descendants
→ dukes
→ Horites
→ kings
→ habitations
```

Genesis 37 immediately returns to an event-bearing household:

```text
Jacob → dwelling relation
Joseph → age / labor relation
Joseph ↔ brothers → flock work
Joseph → report to father
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{population database grammar}
\neq
\text{household event/information grammar}
}
```

The representation level must change with the text.

## Joseph's report is a report object

The text says:

```text
Joseph → father
object → "their evil report"
```

But it does not specify the report's detailed content in this verse.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
R_{Joseph\rightarrow father}=\text{evil report}
}
```

while:

```math
\boxed{
\text{complete underlying reported event(s)}=\mathrm{OPEN}
}
```

Do not transform `evil report` into a fully reconstructed event history that the chapter does not supply.

The exact scope of `their` should also remain close to the local wording rather than being over-specified beyond the brother-group context.

## Unequal parental love is explicitly narrated

The text gives:

```text
Israel → loves Joseph more than all his children
explicit reason → Joseph is son of his old age
Israel → makes Joseph coat of many colours
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{parental preference}
\neq
\text{reason given for preference}
\neq
\text{coat-making action}
}
```

The coat is a physical object associated with the differentiated relationship, but the text does not need the parser to assign it a complete symbolic theory.

## Brothers observe the preference and react

The sequence is explicit:

```text
brothers → see father loves Joseph more
↓
brothers → hate Joseph
↓
brothers → cannot speak peaceably unto him
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{observed parental preference}
\rightarrow
\text{hatred}
\rightarrow
\text{changed communication relation}
}
```

But:

```math
\boxed{
\text{hatred}
\neq
\text{later conspiracy}
}
```

The later action state has not yet been narrated.

## Jacob / Israel labels remain context-sensitive

Verse 1 uses `Jacob`; verse 3 uses `Israel`.

As established in Genesis 32 and 35:

```math
\boxed{
\text{renaming}
\neq
\text{global erasure of prior label}
}
```

Both surface labels continue to refer within the narrative without requiring one to be deleted from the representation.

# 2. Genesis 37:5–11 — Dreams, reports, interpretations, hatred, envy, rebuke, and observation

## Plain rendering

Joseph dreams a dream and tells it to his brothers; they hate him still more. Joseph reports the first dream: the brothers are binding sheaves, Joseph's sheaf rises and stands upright, and the brothers' sheaves stand around and bow to Joseph's sheaf. The brothers interpret the report through questions about Joseph reigning over or having dominion over them and hate him further because of his dreams and words. Joseph dreams a second dream and reports it to his brothers: the sun, moon, and eleven stars bow to him. He tells it to his father and brothers. His father rebukes him and interprets the dream as himself, Joseph's mother, and Joseph's brothers bowing to Joseph. The brothers envy Joseph; his father observes the saying.

## Dream event ≠ dream report

First:

```text
Joseph → dreams
Joseph → tells brothers
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
D_{Joseph}
\neq
R_{Joseph}(D_{Joseph})
}
```

The dream is Joseph's experienced dream-state content.

The report is the communicated representation available to the brothers.

## First dream content

The reported dream gives:

```text
Joseph + brothers → binding sheaves
Joseph's sheaf → rises
Joseph's sheaf → stands upright
brothers' sheaves → surround Joseph's
brothers' sheaves → make obeisance to Joseph's
```

The brothers then generate an interpretation:

```text
Joseph → reign over us?
Joseph → have dominion over us?
```

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{sheaf dream content}
\neq
\text{brothers' reign/dominion interpretation}
}
```

The interpretation is supplied by the recipients.

The chapter does not yet narrate a later reign/dominion event.

## Hatred accumulates through multiple represented causes

The chapter gives at least:

```text
v4 → hatred after seeing father's preference
v5 → greater hatred after dream report
v8 → greater hatred for dreams + words
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
H_{v4}
\rightarrow
H_{v5}
\rightarrow
H_{v8}
}
```

as repeated/intensified narrated states.

Do not compress all hatred references into one timeless property and lose their local triggers.

## Second dream and wider audience

Joseph reports:

```text
sun
moon
11 stars
→ make obeisance to Joseph
```

The audience expands:

```text
brothers → hear
father → hears
```

Jacob/Israel's response contains two different operations:

```text
father → rebukes Joseph
father → interprets dream
```

His interpretation is:

```text
I
+ thy mother
+ thy brethren
→ bow to thee
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{dream image}
\neq
R_{father}(\text{dream referents})
}
```

## Rachel-death continuity caution

Genesis 35 has already narrated Rachel's death.

Genesis 37 records the father's speech:

```text
"I and thy mother and thy brethren"
```

The structural layer should preserve both records:

```math
\boxed{
G35:\ \text{Rachel dies}
}
```

and:

```math
\boxed{
G37:\ R_{father}(\text{dream})\text{ includes `thy mother`}
}
```

without silently rewriting either one.

The exact referent/force of `thy mother` inside the dream interpretation can remain OPEN beyond the local speech.

## Different recipient reactions

After the second dream:

```text
brothers → envy Joseph
father → observes the saying
```

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
R_{brothers}(\text{dream encounter})
\neq
R_{father}(\text{dream encounter})
}
```

and:

```math
\boxed{
\text{envy}
\neq
\text{observing/retaining the saying}
}
```

The exact cognitive content of `observed the saying` should remain close to the source wording.

# 3. Genesis 37:12–17 — Delegated search, changing location information, and third-party update

## Plain rendering

Joseph's brothers go to feed their father's flock in Shechem. Israel sends Joseph to see whether his brothers and the flocks are well and to bring word back. Joseph leaves the vale of Hebron and reaches Shechem. A certain man finds Joseph wandering in the field, asks what he seeks, and Joseph says he seeks his brothers and asks where they feed their flocks. The man reports that they have departed and says he heard them say they would go to Dothan. Joseph follows and finds his brothers at Dothan.

## Delegated mission

Israel gives Joseph a task with several components:

```text
Joseph → go
Joseph → inspect brothers' welfare
Joseph → inspect flock welfare
Joseph → bring word back
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{movement instruction}
+
\text{observation objective}
+
\text{return-report objective}
}
```

The intended information loop is:

```math
\boxed{
Israel
\rightarrow
Joseph
\rightarrow
\text{brothers/flocks}
\rightarrow
Joseph observation
\rightarrow
R_{Joseph\rightarrow Israel}
}
```

But Genesis 37 will break that intended loop before Joseph returns.

## Initial destination ≠ actual brother location

Joseph reaches Shechem, but the brothers are no longer there.

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{expected/search location}
\neq
\text{actual target location}
}
```

## Third-party information bridge

A `certain man`:

```text
finds Joseph wandering
asks what he seeks
```

Joseph discloses:

```text
objective → find brothers
question → where do they feed flocks?
```

The man responds:

```text
brothers → departed Shechem
man → heard brothers say `Let us go to Dothan`
```

This is not direct current observation of the brothers at Dothan.

It is a report based on overheard prior speech:

```math
\boxed{
\text{brothers' prior speech}
\rightarrow
I_{man}
\rightarrow
R_{man\rightarrow Joseph}
\rightarrow
I_{Joseph}
}
```

Joseph then acts on that information:

```text
Joseph → goes after brothers
Joseph → finds them in Dothan
```

Thus the report receives later local confirmation at the location level.

# 4. Genesis 37:18–22 — Brothers see Joseph first; concealed kill plan; Reuben changes the public plan while holding a different private objective

## Plain rendering

The brothers see Joseph from far away before he comes near. They conspire to kill him. They call him `this dreamer` and propose killing him, throwing him into a pit, and later saying that an evil beast devoured him. Reuben hears the plan, intervenes, says not to kill Joseph, and proposes putting him into the wilderness pit without shedding blood or laying hand on him. The narrator separately supplies Reuben's purpose: to rescue Joseph from their hands and return him to his father.

## Information asymmetry begins before physical contact

The brothers:

```text
see Joseph afar off
↓
conspire before he comes near
```

Joseph's corresponding knowledge of that conspiracy is not narrated.

So at minimum:

```math
\boxed{
I_{brothers}(\text{conspiracy})
\neq
I_{Joseph}(\text{conspiracy})
}
```

Joseph is physically approaching under the earlier delegated mission.

The brothers are already acting under a new hidden plan.

## Initial kill + cover-story proposal

The brothers' first stated plan contains both an intended world action and an intended later representation:

```text
kill Joseph
↓
cast him into pit
↓
later say:
    evil beast devoured him
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{planned harm}
+
\text{planned false explanatory report}
}
```

This is stronger than merely inferring that the brothers later wished Jacob to believe Joseph dead: the initial dialogue explicitly includes a proposed false story.

But:

```math
\boxed{
\text{initially proposed cover story}
\neq
\text{exact later words delivered to Jacob}
}
```

The final evidence-presentation procedure will be different.

## Dream relation enters the plan

The brothers say:

```text
"we shall see what will become of his dreams"
```

This is their own relation between the proposed action and Joseph's prior dream reports.

Do not upgrade it to a narrator-level mechanism concerning dream realization.

## Reuben's intervention contains public and private layers

Reuben hears the plan.

His communicated proposal is:

```text
do not kill Joseph
shed no blood
put him in pit
lay no hand upon him
```

But the narrator separately tells the reader Reuben's intended downstream action:

```text
rid/deliver Joseph from brothers' hands
→ restore Joseph to father
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
R_{Reuben\rightarrow brothers}(\text{pit proposal})
\neq
I_{reader}(\text{Reuben's full rescue objective})
}
```

This is an important mirror to Genesis 34:

```text
Genesis 34 → deceptive public condition + hidden hostile divergence
Genesis 37 → public pit proposal + hidden rescue objective
```

The general structural primitive is neutral:

```math
\boxed{
\text{communicated proposal}
\neq
\text{complete private objective}
}
```

The moral direction of the hidden objective differs by case.

# 5. Genesis 37:23–25 — Joseph arrives; coat removed; pit confinement; brothers eat; new external opportunity appears

## Plain rendering

When Joseph reaches his brothers, they strip him of his coat of many colours, take him, and throw him into the pit. The text specifies that the pit is empty and has no water. The brothers sit down to eat. They then see a company of Ishmeelites coming from Gilead with camels carrying spicery, balm, and myrrh toward Egypt.

## Coat state transition

Earlier:

```text
Israel → makes coat for Joseph
Joseph → wears coat
```

Now:

```text
brothers → strip coat from Joseph
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{status-associated object possessed/worn by Joseph}
\rightarrow
\text{object separated from Joseph}
}
```

The coat persists even as Joseph's physical status changes.

That persistence becomes crucial later because the object can be manipulated and presented independently of Joseph.

## Pit state

```text
Joseph → taken
Joseph → cast into pit
pit → empty
pit → no water
```

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{conspiracy}
\neq
\text{coat stripping}
\neq
\text{physical confinement in pit}
}
```

And the source explicitly gives pit properties rather than leaving them to inference.

## Brothers' activity changes while Joseph remains confined

```text
brothers → sit down
brothers → eat bread
```

Then:

```text
brothers → lift eyes / look
brothers → observe Ishmeelite company
```

The caravan becomes new information that changes the available action space.

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{new environmental/social observation}
\rightarrow
\text{new proposal opportunity}
}
```

but the proposal itself does not appear until Judah speaks.

# 6. Genesis 37:26–30 — Judah proposes sale; brothers accept; extraction/sale occurs; Reuben later discovers Joseph absent

## Plain rendering

Judah asks what profit there is in killing their brother and concealing his blood. He proposes selling Joseph to the Ishmeelites rather than laying their hand on him, noting that Joseph is their brother and flesh. The brothers agree. Midianite merchantmen pass by; the text then says `they` draw and lift Joseph from the pit, sell Joseph to the Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver, and bring Joseph into Egypt. Reuben later returns to the pit, sees Joseph is absent, rends his clothes, returns to his brothers, and asks where he can go.

## Plan evolution

The chapter has now earned a multi-stage plan graph:

```text
initial brother plan:
    kill
    pit
    false beast report

Reuben intervention:
    no killing
    pit
    narrator-known rescue objective

performed intermediate state:
    strip coat
    pit Joseph

Judah proposal after caravan appears:
    sell Joseph
    do not kill

brothers:
    content / accept
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{initial plan}
\neq
\text{intervening proposal}
\neq
\text{intermediate implementation}
\neq
\text{revised proposal}
\neq
\text{final physical transfer}
}
```

Plans are stateful objects in the chapter; they change before the trajectory is completed.

## Judah's explicit reasoning

Judah gives:

```text
question → what profit if kill + conceal blood?
proposal → sell to Ishmeelites
constraint → let not our hand be upon him
reason → brother / our flesh
```

Keep these as Judah's stated reasons rather than replacing them with a generalized motive theory.

## Midianite / Ishmeelite actor-label caution

Verse 25 had introduced:

```text
company of Ishmeelites
```

Verse 28 then says:

```text
Midianites merchantmen → pass by
"they" → draw/lift Joseph from pit
"they" → sell Joseph to Ishmeelites
"they" → bring Joseph into Egypt
```

Verse 36 later says:

```text
Midianites → sell Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar
```

Therefore the structural layer should not silently flatten all labels or pronouns into:

```text
brothers personally draw Joseph out
brothers personally receive silver
brothers personally sell directly to final Egyptian owner
```

The text has multiple group labels and a locally ambiguous pronoun chain.

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{brothers accept Judah's sale proposal}
\neq
\text{fully resolved actor mapping for every verb in v28}
}
```

and:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Ishmeelites}
\neq
\text{Midianites}
}
```

at the surface-label level unless later evidence earns a stronger mapping.

## Price relation

The text gives:

```math
\boxed{20\ \text{pieces of silver}}
```

as the sale price.

But the exact recipient allocation of the silver is not separately narrated in the verse.

Do not invent a full financial distribution graph.

## Joseph's physical state changes again

```text
pit-confined Joseph
→ drawn/lifted out
→ sold/transferred
→ transported toward/into Egypt
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{pit release}
\neq
\text{return to household freedom}
}
```

Release from one constraint is immediately attached to a different transfer state.

## Reuben discovers rather than already knows

The sequence is:

```text
Reuben → returns to pit
↓
sees Joseph absent
↓
rends clothes
↓
returns to brothers
↓
"The child is not"
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
I_{Reuben}^{before\ return}
\neq
I_{Reuben}^{after\ observing\ empty\ pit}
}
```

The chapter does not narrate Reuben as already informed of the sale/removal before his return.

The exact reason Reuben was absent from the pit during the extraction/sale is not given.

```math
\boxed{
\text{Reuben's intervening whereabouts}=\mathrm{OPEN}
}
```

# 7. Genesis 37:31–33 — The coat becomes manipulated evidence; Jacob supplies the false death proposition

## Plain rendering

The brothers take Joseph's coat, kill a kid of the goats, and dip the coat in blood. They send the coat of many colours and bring it to their father. They say, `This have we found: know now whether it be thy son's coat or no.` Jacob recognizes it and says it is his son's coat. Jacob then infers that an evil beast has devoured Joseph and that Joseph is without doubt rent in pieces.

## Evidence production is a multi-stage physical operation

```text
Joseph's coat → retained apart from Joseph
kid of goats → killed
kid blood → available
coat → dipped in blood
coat → sent/brought to Jacob
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{real coat}
+
\text{real blood}
\neq
\text{true provenance relation `Joseph's blood`}
}
```

The physical components are real.

The misleading evidential relation comes from selection, manipulation, provenance omission, and presentation context.

## Initial planned lie ≠ final communicated sentence

Earlier, the brothers had proposed saying:

```text
"Some evil beast hath devoured him"
```

But the final speech to Jacob is:

```text
"This have we found"
"know now whether it be thy son's coat or no"
```

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
R_{planned,v20}
\neq
R_{delivered,v32}
}
```

The later procedure does not directly utter the death proposition.

## Recognition is locally accurate

Jacob examines/recognizes the object and concludes:

```text
it is my son's coat
```

That object-identification is correct relative to the narrator's prior record.

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{correct object recognition}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{correct event inference}
}
```

This is a major structural point.

Jacob correctly identifies the coat but has incomplete/false provenance information about the blood and about how the coat became separated from Joseph.

## Jacob generates the death interpretation

Jacob then says:

```text
evil beast → devoured Joseph
Joseph → without doubt rent in pieces
```

The chapter does not narrate the presenters directly saying either sentence to Jacob at this moment.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{manipulated evidence presentation}
\rightarrow
\text{recipient object recognition}
\rightarrow
\text{recipient causal/event inference}
}
```

and:

```math
\boxed{
\textbf{false belief production does not require direct utterance of the false proposition.}
}
```

The chapter demonstrates a route through:

```text
selection of object
+ physical manipulation
+ omitted provenance
+ contextual presentation
+ recipient inference
```

without requiring modern terminology to describe it.

## Jacob's inferred beast story matches the brothers' earlier proposed story

This correspondence is exact enough to record:

```text
brothers' v20 planned story → evil beast devoured Joseph
Jacob's v33 inference → evil beast devoured Joseph
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
R_{brothers,planned}
\leftrightarrow
R_{Jacob,inferred}
}
```

But the final speech does not itself narrate the direct transmission of that proposition.

Thus the correspondence does not erase the information mechanism.

# 8. Genesis 37:34–36 — False belief produces real grief while Joseph remains alive and reaches a new social state in Egypt

## Plain rendering

Jacob rends his clothes, puts on sackcloth, and mourns for Joseph many days. His sons and daughters try to comfort him; he refuses comfort and says he will go to the grave mourning for his son. His father weeps for him. Meanwhile, the Midianites sell Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard.

## Jacob's emotional/behavioral state is real even though the death belief is false

```text
Jacob → believes Joseph dead
↓
Jacob → rends clothes
Jacob → sackcloth
Jacob → mourns many days
sons+daughters → attempt comfort
Jacob → refuses comfort
Jacob → represents lifelong/until-grave mourning trajectory
Jacob → weeps
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{false world-model proposition}
\rightarrow
\text{real agent state change}
}
```

The grief does not become unreal merely because its causal belief is wrong.

This distinction is especially important:

```math
\boxed{
\text{belief accuracy}
\neq
\text{causal efficacy of belief on agent behavior/emotion}
}
```

## `many days` is a persistence marker

The chapter does not merely narrate a momentary reaction.

It says:

```text
Jacob → mourned many days
```

Thus the false-information state has durable within-chapter consequences.

Do not infer the exact duration beyond the source wording.

## Comfort attempt ≠ successful comfort

```text
sons + daughters → rise to comfort Jacob
Jacob → refuses to be comforted
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{support attempt}
\neq
\text{state correction}
}
```

The household cannot repair Jacob's represented event history through the comfort attempt narrated here.

## Parallel world states at chapter end

Jacob's represented state:

```text
Joseph → dead / devoured / rent in pieces
```

Reader/narrator world state:

```text
Joseph → alive
Joseph → Egypt
Joseph → sold to Potiphar
Potiphar → officer of Pharaoh
Potiphar → captain of guard
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
W_{world}(Joseph=alive)
\neq
I_{Jacob}(Joseph=dead)
}
```

while the brothers possess information not available to Jacob about Joseph's removal from the household.

The chapter therefore closes on a deliberately forked information topology rather than a shared family state.


```text
↓
brothers → hatred
```

```text
Joseph dream-state content
↓
Joseph reports to brothers
↓
brothers interpret reign/dominion
↓
hatred increases

↓
Joseph reports to brothers + father
↓
father interprets family bowing
brothers → envy
```

## Search layer

```text
Israel → thinks brothers at/associated with Shechem
↓
Joseph → sent to inspect + report
↓
Joseph reaches Shechem
↓
```

# 13. OPEN edges / non-inferences

## OPEN 1 — Detailed content of Joseph's `evil report`

The report is explicitly characterized as evil, but the underlying event details are not supplied here.

## OPEN 2 — Exact scope of `their` in `their evil report`

Preserve the local brother-group context without inventing finer referent precision than the text provides.

## OPEN 3 — Ontology of dream content

The dreams are narrated as dreams and then interpreted by recipients.

Do not pre-promote dream imagery into already-realized future events.

## OPEN 4 — Exact referent/force of `thy mother` in the father's second-dream interpretation

Genesis 35 has narrated Rachel's death. Genesis 37 still gives the father's speech exactly as written. Preserve both.

## OPEN 5 — Joseph's awareness of the conspiracy before capture

The brothers conspire before Joseph comes near; Joseph's knowledge of that plan is not narrated.

## OPEN 6 — Reuben's whereabouts during Joseph's extraction/sale

The chapter later says Reuben returned to the pit and discovered Joseph absent. It does not narrate where Reuben was during the intervening transfer.

## OPEN 7 — Exact actor resolution in Genesis 37:28

The verse introduces `Midianites merchantmen` and then uses `they` for drawing, lifting, selling, and bringing Joseph. Avoid silently resolving every verb to the brothers.

## OPEN 8 — Relationship between `Ishmeelites` and `Midianites`

Both labels occur in the chapter. Preserve the surface labels unless a later explicit bridge resolves them.

## OPEN 9 — Recipient/distribution of the twenty pieces of silver

The sale price is supplied; the complete financial allocation is not.

## OPEN 10 — Exact mechanics by which the bloodied coat produces Jacob's inference

The object is presented with an identification question; Jacob recognizes and infers. The chapter does not provide a psychological model beyond that sequence.

## OPEN 11 — Brothers' exact information partition after the sale

The chapter gives Reuben's later surprise and the broader brother-group action around the coat, but does not itemize every brother's knowledge state at every instant.

## OPEN 12 — Duration of Jacob's false belief beyond the chapter

Genesis 37 shows mourning for many days and closes with Joseph alive in Egypt. Later chapters may change Jacob's information state; do not import that update backward.
