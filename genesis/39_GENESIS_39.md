# Genesis 39 — Legibility-First Structural Decoding

**Source:** pinned King James Version (KJV) corpus  
**Purpose:** make the chapter legible before interpretation  
**Status:** working structural reading; categories may evolve when later text requires them

The governing boundary remains:

```math
\boxed{\text{TEXT} \neq \text{STRUCTURAL PARSE} \neq \text{INTERPRETATION}}
```

and the working question for this chapter is:

```math
\boxed{\textbf{How does Genesis 39 represent trust, delegated authority, boundary violation, attempted action, physical evidence, accusatory representation, and state transition?}}
```

Genesis 39 resumes Joseph's Egyptian trajectory after the Judah-centered interruption of Genesis 38. Joseph is bought by Potiphar and enters a new institutional environment as a servant. The narrator says the LORD is with Joseph and makes what Joseph does prosper; Potiphar sees this and progressively commits his house and possessions into Joseph's hand. Joseph's social and functional position therefore changes from purchased servant to overseer under broad delegated authority. Joseph then states one explicit relational exclusion within that authority: Potiphar has kept back his wife because she is his wife.

Potiphar's wife repeatedly tells Joseph to lie with her. Joseph refuses and supplies his own reasons, including the trust delegated by his master, the wife's excluded status, the act's wickedness, and sin against God. On a later occasion Joseph enters the house to do his business while none of the men of the house are present within. The wife catches him by his garment and repeats the demand. Joseph leaves the garment in her hand, flees, and gets out.

The garment then changes function. It had been clothing physically attached to Joseph; after the encounter it remains in the wife's hand. She calls the men of the house and gives an account in which Joseph came in to lie with her, she cried loudly, and he left the garment and fled when he heard the cry. She retains the garment until Potiphar returns and gives a corresponding accusation to him. The primary narration and the later accusation do not supply the same event sequence or causal provenance for the garment. Potiphar hears her words, his wrath is kindled, and he puts Joseph in the prison where the king's prisoners are bound. The chapter does not explicitly state Potiphar's internal belief proposition; it gives hearing, anger, and action.

Inside prison, the delegation pattern returns. The narrator again says the LORD is with Joseph; the keeper gives Joseph favour and commits the prisoners and prison activity into his hand. The keeper does not look to what is under Joseph's hand, and the narrator again attributes Joseph's prosperity to the LORD's presence.

A first-pass chapter grammar is therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Joseph brought to Egypt}
\rightarrow
\text{purchase / servant state}
\rightarrow
\text{LORD-with-Joseph + prosperity}
\rightarrow
\text{Potiphar observation}
\rightarrow
\text{progressive delegated authority}
\rightarrow
\text{explicit relational boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{repeated proposition}
\rightarrow
\text{repeated refusal}
\rightarrow
\text{isolated house context}
\rightarrow
\text{garment seizure + repeated proposition}
\rightarrow
\text{garment abandonment + flight}
\rightarrow
\text{garment retention}
\rightarrow
\text{audience-specific accusatory reports}
\rightarrow
\text{master hearing + wrath}
\rightarrow
\text{prison placement}
\rightarrow
\text{LORD-with-Joseph + favour}
\rightarrow
\text{renewed delegated authority}
\rightarrow
\text{continued prosperity}
}
```

The main disciplines for this chapter are:

```math
\boxed{
\text{person identity}
\neq
\text{formal social status}
\neq
\text{delegated functional authority}
}
```

```math
\boxed{
\text{proposition}
\neq
\text{refusal}
\neq
\text{physical seizure}
\neq
\text{completed sexual act}
}
```

```math
\boxed{
\text{genuine garment}
\neq
\text{true provenance of garment state}
\neq
\text{reported provenance/event reconstruction}
}
```

```math
\boxed{
\text{hearing accusation}
\neq
\text{internal belief explicitly narrated}
\neq
\text{institutional action}
}
```

and:

```math
\boxed{
\text{formal demotion / imprisonment}
\neq
\text{loss of functional capability}
}
```

# 1. Genesis 39:1 — Joseph's Egyptian acquisition record

## Plain rendering

Joseph is brought down to Egypt. Potiphar—an officer of Pharaoh, captain of the guard, and an Egyptian—buys Joseph from the hands of the Ishmeelites who had brought Joseph there.

## Joseph's person persists across a status transition

Genesis 37 ended with Joseph in Egypt and sold to Potiphar.

Genesis 39 opens:

```text
Joseph → brought down to Egypt
Potiphar → buys Joseph
Joseph → enters Potiphar's household relation
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{persistent person Joseph}
\neq
\text{new legal/social/economic status relation}
}
```

The text represents Joseph as the same person while his external relation changes from son/brother in Canaan to purchased servant in Egypt.

## Potiphar's role labels

The chapter gives multiple properties:

```text
Potiphar → officer of Pharaoh
Potiphar → captain of guard
Potiphar → Egyptian
Potiphar → Joseph's purchaser/master
```

These are distinct relation/status fields rather than one generic `authority` property.

## Genesis 37:36 / Genesis 39:1 surface-record tension

Genesis 37:36 says:

```text
Midianites → sold Joseph in Egypt unto Potiphar
```

Genesis 39:1 says:

```text
Potiphar → bought Joseph from hands of Ishmeelites
Ishmeelites → had brought Joseph down to Egypt
```

Therefore preserve both source records:

```math
\boxed{
R_{G37:36}(\text{seller}=\text{Midianites})
\neq
R_{G39:1}(\text{seller-source}=\text{Ishmeelites})
}
```

Do not silently harmonize Midianite/Ishmeelite actor labels or invent an undocumented transaction chain.

The exact mapping among the caravan labels and sale stages remains:

```math
\boxed{\mathrm{OPEN}}
```

at the structural layer.

# 2. Genesis 39:2–6 — Prosperity, observation, grace, service, and progressive delegation

## Plain rendering

The LORD is with Joseph, and Joseph is a prosperous man in the house of his Egyptian master. Potiphar sees that the LORD is with Joseph and that the LORD makes all Joseph does prosper in his hand. Joseph finds grace in Potiphar's sight and serves him. Potiphar makes Joseph overseer over his house and puts all he has into Joseph's hand. From that time, the LORD blesses the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake, in house and field. Potiphar leaves all he has in Joseph's hand and knows not what he has except the bread he eats. Joseph is described as goodly and well favoured.

## Narrator-level prosperity relation

The text explicitly supplies:

```text
LORD → with Joseph
Joseph → prosperous
LORD → makes all Joseph does prosper
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Joseph activity}
\neq
\text{prosperous outcome}
\neq
\text{narrator-supplied LORD attribution}
}
```

The structural layer should preserve the narrator's stated causal attribution without adding a detailed mechanism beyond the text.

## Potiphar observes a state

The text says:

```text
Potiphar → saw LORD was with Joseph
Potiphar → saw LORD made all Joseph did prosper
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{world/narrator state}
\rightarrow
\text{Potiphar observation}
}
```

But the exact observation interface by which Potiphar identifies `the LORD was with him` is not specified.

```math
\boxed{
\text{Potiphar's observation claim}
\neq
\text{mechanism of recognition supplied}
}
```

## Delegation is progressive and typed

The text gives a progression:

```text
Joseph → servant
Joseph → finds grace
Potiphar → makes Joseph overseer over house
Potiphar → puts all he has into Joseph's hand
Potiphar → leaves all he has in Joseph's hand
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{servant status}
\rightarrow
\text{trusted service relation}
\rightarrow
\text{overseer role}
\rightarrow
\text{broad delegated resource authority}
}
```

But:

```math
\boxed{
\text{delegated control}
\neq
\text{ownership}
}
```

The possessions remain Potiphar's; they are committed into Joseph's hand.

## Delegation changes Potiphar's attention/information burden

Verse 6 says Potiphar:

```text
left all he had in Joseph's hand
knew not ought he had
save → bread which he did eat
```

This supports:

```math
\boxed{
\text{delegated responsibility}
\rightarrow
\text{reduced directly attended household detail for Potiphar}
}
```

without needing to interpret the phrase as absolute ignorance of every possession.

Preserve the exact textual scope.

## Household benefit extends beyond Joseph alone

The narrator says:

```text
from Joseph's overseer appointment
→ LORD blesses Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake
blessing → house
blessing → field
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Joseph local role}
\rightarrow
\text{narrated wider household/resource benefit}
}
```

The chapter itself supplies this relation.

## Joseph's appearance is a separate property

The chapter then says:

```text
Joseph → goodly person
Joseph → well favoured
```

This property is adjacent to the later wife's attention but should remain a separate narrator-description unless the text explicitly connects every later action causally to it.

# 3. Genesis 39:7–10 — Repeated proposition, explicit refusal, and Joseph's represented boundary

## Plain rendering

Potiphar's wife casts her eyes upon Joseph and says, "Lie with me." Joseph refuses. He explains that his master does not attend to what is with Joseph in the house, has committed everything to Joseph's hand, has made Joseph greatest in the house, and has withheld nothing except his wife because she is his wife. Joseph asks how he could do this great wickedness and sin against God. The wife speaks to Joseph day by day, but Joseph does not hearken to her, either to lie by her or to be with her.

## Initial proposition

The text gives:

```text
master's wife → casts eyes on Joseph
master's wife → says "Lie with me"
Joseph → refuses
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{attention/desire signal}
\neq
\text{spoken proposition}
\neq
\text{Joseph response}
}
```

## Joseph's boundary representation

Joseph's own speech gives the local authority map:

```text
master → committed all he has to Joseph's hand
Joseph → none greater in house
master → kept back nothing except wife
reason → she is master's wife
```

Thus Joseph represents:

```math
\boxed{
A_{Joseph}^{delegated}
=
\text{broad household domain}
\setminus
\{\text{master's wife relation}\}
}
```

This is not merely a parser-imposed boundary. Joseph explicitly states the exclusion.

## Joseph's reasons are themselves data

Joseph says:

```text
act → great wickedness
act → sin against God
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
R_{Joseph}(\text{reasons for refusal})
=
\{\text{delegated trust boundary},\text{wife relation},\text{wickedness},\text{sin against God}\}
}
```

The structural layer should preserve these stated reasons without reducing them to one external moral theory.

## Repetition matters

Verse 10 says:

```text
wife → speaks to Joseph day by day
Joseph → hearkens not
Joseph → does not lie by her
Joseph → does not be with her
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{single refusal}
\neq
\text{persistent repeated refusal state}
}
```

And the chapter distinguishes:

```text
lie by her
be with her
```

as surface clauses; do not collapse their exact local scope unnecessarily.

# 4. Genesis 39:11–12 — Isolated setting, garment seizure, and flight

## Plain rendering

At a later time Joseph enters the house to do his business. None of the men of the house are there within. Potiphar's wife catches Joseph by his garment and again says, "Lie with me." Joseph leaves the garment in her hand, flees, and gets out.

## Joseph's purpose on entry is explicit

The text says:

```text
Joseph → enters house
purpose → do his business
```

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Joseph enters house}
\neq
\text{Joseph enters for sexual encounter}
}
```

The latter is not the primary narrated purpose.

## Local witness environment changes

The narrator says:

```text
none of men of house → there within
```

This gives a specific local observation/witness state:

```math
\boxed{
\text{house context at encounter}
=
\text{no men of house present within}
}
```

Do not inflate this to `no possible witness anywhere` beyond the verse's wording.

## Physical action sequence

The text gives:

```text
wife → catches Joseph by garment
wife → says "Lie with me"
Joseph → leaves garment in her hand
Joseph → flees
Joseph → gets out
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{garment seizure}
\neq
\text{repeated proposition}
\neq
\text{Joseph abandonment of garment}
\neq
\text{flight}
}
```

No completed sexual act is narrated.

## Garment state transition

Before verse 12:

```text
garment → on/with Joseph
```

During encounter:

```text
wife → catches garment
Joseph → leaves garment
```

After encounter:

```text
garment → in wife's hand
Joseph → physically absent / fled out
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{person-object attachment}
\rightarrow
\text{seizure}
\rightarrow
\text{object left behind during escape}
}
```

This physical provenance becomes crucial because later speech supplies a different causal account for why the garment is in her possession.

# 5. Genesis 39:13–18 — The garment becomes an accusation object; the event is re-represented to two audiences

## Plain rendering

When Potiphar's wife sees that Joseph has left his garment in her hand and fled, she calls the men of the house. She tells them that "he" has brought a Hebrew in "to mock us," says Joseph came in to lie with her, says she cried loudly, and says Joseph left his garment and fled when he heard her cry. She keeps the garment beside her until Potiphar returns. She then tells Potiphar according to these words, calling Joseph "the Hebrew servant, which thou hast brought unto us," saying he came to mock her, and again says that when she cried Joseph left the garment and fled.

## Observation before report

The wife first observes:

```text
Joseph → garment left in her hand
Joseph → fled forth
```

Then she constructs reports.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{observed aftermath}
\neq
\text{reported causal history of aftermath}
}
```

## Primary narration versus wife's first report

Primary narration:

```text
wife catches garment
wife says lie with me
Joseph leaves garment
Joseph flees
```

Wife's report to household men:

```text
Joseph came in to lie with me
I cried with loud voice
Joseph heard cry
Joseph left garment with me
Joseph fled
```

The primary narration does not narrate her crying before Joseph's flight.

So:

```math
\boxed{
E_{primary}
\neq
R_{wife\rightarrow household}(E)
}
```

More specifically:

```math
\boxed{
\text{garment true provenance in narration}
=
\text{wife seizure + Joseph flight}
}
```

whereas:

```math
\boxed{
R_{wife}(\text{garment provenance})
=
\text{Joseph sexual approach + wife's cry + Joseph flight}
}
```

The object is genuine; the reported event history attached to it diverges from the primary narration.

## Audience-specific framing: household servants

To the men of the house she says:

```text
"he hath brought in an Hebrew unto us to mock us"
```

The local referent of `he` is naturally linked to the absent household master in the speech context, but the structural layer should preserve the wording rather than over-formalize unspoken blame mechanics.

She frames Joseph as:

```text
Hebrew
outsider brought "unto us"
source of mockery toward "us"
```

So the report is not merely an event proposition; it also contains audience-relative social framing.

## Garment retained through time

Verse 16 says:

```text
wife → lays garment by her
until → lord comes home
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{object retained}
\rightarrow
\text{delayed second presentation/report}
}
```

The garment becomes a persistent bridge between the encounter and later institutional consequence.

## Audience-specific framing: Potiphar

To Potiphar she says:

```text
"The Hebrew servant, which thou hast brought unto us"
→ came in to mock me
I cried
he left garment
he fled
```

This overlaps strongly with the household report but is not textually identical.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
R_{wife\rightarrow household}(E)
\neq
R_{wife\rightarrow Potiphar}(E)
}
```

while:

```math
\boxed{
\text{core accusatory structure overlaps}
}
```

This is another instance of later speech being an additional representation, not a transparent substitute for the primary event record.

## Joseph's account is absent from the accusation scene

Joseph is not narrated giving a counter-report to the household servants or Potiphar in verses 13–20.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{wife testimony available to Potiphar}
\neq
\text{two-sided testimony scene}
}
```

The structural layer knows Joseph's earlier refusal and flight from primary narration, but should not invent an unrecorded defense speech.

# 6. Genesis 39:19–20 — Potiphar hears, wrath is kindled, and Joseph is imprisoned

## Plain rendering

Potiphar hears the words of his wife, including her statement about what his servant did to her. His wrath is kindled. Potiphar takes Joseph and puts him in the prison, a place where the king's prisoners are bound. Joseph is there in the prison.

## Hearing is explicit; belief is not

The chapter says:

```text
Potiphar → hears wife's words
Potiphar → wrath kindled
Potiphar → takes Joseph
Potiphar → puts Joseph in prison
```

It does not explicitly say:

```text
Potiphar → believed every proposition in wife's report
```

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{report received}
\neq
\text{belief proposition explicitly narrated}
\neq
\text{institutional action}
}
```

The safest information-state representation is:

```math
\boxed{
I_{Potiphar}:\text{wife's accusation received}
}
```

with:

```math
\boxed{
B_{Potiphar}(\text{full accusation})=\mathrm{OPEN}
}
```

and observed response:

```math
\boxed{
\text{wrath + prison placement}
}
```

## Wrath target caution

The text says simply:

```text
his wrath was kindled
```

The following action is against Joseph, but the grammatical clause does not separately label the target of the wrath.

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{wrath state explicit}
\neq
\text{complete internal target model explicit}
}
```

## Institutional state change

Joseph moves from:

```text
Potiphar's household overseer
```

to:

```text
prisoner located in prison
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
S_{Joseph}^{formal}:
\text{trusted overseer}
\rightarrow
\text{prisoner}
}
```

This is a real status collapse even though Joseph's underlying person identity persists.

## Prison-description caution

The prison is described as:

```text
place where king's prisoners were bound
```

The verse then says Joseph was there in prison.

Do not automatically infer a separately narrated chain/binding event for Joseph from the general facility description.

```math
\boxed{
\text{facility contains bound king's prisoners}
\neq
\text{Joseph binding mechanism individually narrated}
}
```

# 7. Genesis 39:21–23 — Prison context reproduces favour, delegation, and prosperity

## Plain rendering

The LORD is with Joseph, shows him mercy, and gives him favour in the sight of the prison keeper. The keeper commits all the prisoners into Joseph's hand, and whatever is done there, Joseph is the doer of it. The keeper does not look to anything under Joseph's hand because the LORD is with Joseph, and the LORD makes what Joseph does prosper.

## Formal prisoner status coexists with delegated responsibility

The chapter now gives:

```text
Joseph → prisoner
Joseph → favour with keeper
keeper → commits all prisoners into Joseph's hand
Joseph → operational responsibility for prison activity
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{formal prisoner status}
\neq
\text{functional authority/responsibility inside prison}
}
```

This is one of the chapter's strongest structural distinctions.

## Delegation pattern recurs across institutions

Potiphar's house:

```text
LORD with Joseph
→ prosperity
→ superior favour/observation
→ resources committed into Joseph's hand
→ superior reduces direct oversight
```

Prison:

```text
LORD with Joseph
→ mercy/favour
→ prisoners committed into Joseph's hand
→ keeper reduces direct oversight
→ prosperity
```

Thus:

```math
\boxed{
\text{household context}
\neq
\text{prison context}
}
```

while:

```math
\boxed{
\text{delegation pattern recurs}
}
```

Do not turn recurrence into a universal theory yet; preserve the repeated local form.

## Keeper's reduced oversight

Verse 23 says:

```text
keeper → looked not to anything under Joseph's hand
reason → LORD was with Joseph
what Joseph did → LORD made prosper
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{delegated scope}
\rightarrow
\text{reduced superior inspection}
}
```

is explicitly present again.

The chapter therefore closes not with Joseph restored to his prior formal status, but with a new combination:

```math
\boxed{
\text{low formal status}
+
\text{high local functional responsibility}
}
```

# 9. Garment provenance ledger

Genesis 39 makes a single physical garment carry multiple relational functions.

## Stage 1 — Clothing

```text
garment → physically with/on Joseph
```

## Stage 2 — Seized object

```text
wife → catches Joseph by garment
```

## Stage 3 — Escape residue

```text
Joseph → leaves garment in wife's hand
Joseph → flees
```

The garment's presence with the wife is therefore a true physical consequence of Joseph's escape from her grasp.

## Stage 4 — Retained evidence object

```text
wife → lays garment by her
until → Potiphar returns
```

## Stage 5 — Accusation support

The wife presents/references the garment within a report whose causal history differs from the primary narration.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{genuine object}
+
\text{genuine possession state}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{genuine narrative attached to possession}
}
```

The key distinction is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{object authenticity}
\neq
\text{provenance accuracy}
}
```

This complements Genesis 37 and 38.

# 11. Cross-chapter structural continuities

## Genesis 37 → Genesis 39: Joseph's acquisition record remains label-sensitive

Genesis 37 ends:

```text
Midianites → sell Joseph to Potiphar
```

Genesis 39 opens:

```text
Potiphar → buys Joseph from Ishmeelites
```

This repeats the Genesis 36 lesson:

```math
\boxed{
\text{name/group label recurrence or adjacency}
\neq
\text{permission to collapse actor identities}
}
```

Preserve local records first.

## Genesis 37 → Genesis 39: garment as genuine object under misleading provenance

Genesis 37:

```text
Joseph's genuine coat
+ goat blood
+ selective presentation
→ Jacob correctly recognizes coat
→ Jacob incorrectly infers death event
```

Genesis 39:

```text
Joseph's genuine garment
+ genuine possession by wife
+ altered event account
→ accusation presented
→ Joseph's institutional state changes
```

The shared abstraction is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{authentic physical artifact}
\neq
\text{accurate event reconstruction}
}
```

But the local functions differ:

```text
G37 → death inference
G39 → culpability accusation / institutional consequence
```

## Genesis 38 → Genesis 39: authentic objects can either repair or corrupt represented history

Genesis 38:

```text
real signet/bracelets/staff
+ correct provenance challenge
→ Judah self-recognition
→ representation correction
```

Genesis 39:

```text
real garment
+ event-incongruent provenance narrative
→ accusation
→ Joseph prison placement
```

So:

```math
\boxed{
\text{object authenticity}
\neq
\text{epistemic direction}
}
```

The direction depends on how provenance is represented.

## Genesis 24 / 31 / 34 / 39: audience-specific retelling

Genesis has repeatedly shown that speech to one audience need not be textually identical to speech to another.

Genesis 39 adds:

```text
wife → household men
wife → Potiphar
```

with overlapping but audience-adjusted wording.

Therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\text{same underlying event}
\neq
\text{same report token across audiences}
}
```

## Genesis 20 / 27 / 37 / 39: action under incomplete or altered representation

Genesis 20 showed Abimelech acting under an incomplete wife/sister representation.

Genesis 27 showed Isaac acting under substituted identity signals.

Genesis 37 showed Jacob inferring death from manipulated evidence.

Genesis 39 shows Potiphar taking institutional action after receiving an account that diverges from the primary narrated event.

The recurring structural object is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{world event}
\neq
\text{representation available to decision-maker}
\neq
\text{decision taken under that representation}
}
```

Do not assume identical mechanism across chapters.

# 12. OPEN edges / non-inferences

## OPEN 1 — Midianite / Ishmeelite transaction mapping

Genesis 37:36 and Genesis 39:1 use different group labels for Joseph's sale to Potiphar.

Preserve both. Do not harmonize without an explicit textual bridge.

## OPEN 2 — How Potiphar knew/recognized that the LORD was with Joseph

The text says Potiphar saw it.

The observation mechanism is not specified.

## OPEN 3 — Exact semantics of Potiphar's `knew not ought he had, save the bread`

Preserve the surface claim as reduced direct concern/knowledge under delegation; do not force an absolute epistemic theory beyond the wording.

## OPEN 4 — Full internal state of Potiphar's wife

The chapter narrates repeated propositions, garment seizure, observation of Joseph's flight, and later event-incongruent reports.

It does not provide a complete quoted internal monologue explaining every motive for the accusation.

## OPEN 5 — Cry event in primary narration

The wife's later report says she cried loudly before Joseph fled.

The primary event narration in verses 11–12 does not narrate such a cry.

Do not silently insert the report's event into the primary sequence.

## OPEN 6 — Joseph's unrecorded defense

No direct Joseph counter-testimony to Potiphar is narrated in the accusation scene.

Do not invent one.

## OPEN 7 — Potiphar's belief state

The text gives hearing, wrath, and prison placement.

It does not explicitly say `Potiphar believed his wife's full account`.

## OPEN 8 — Target of Potiphar's wrath

Wrath is explicit; the clause does not separately identify its internal target. Potiphar's subsequent action is against Joseph.

## OPEN 9 — Exact prison-binding state of Joseph

The prison is where the king's prisoners are bound. Joseph is put there.

A separate binding action on Joseph is not narrated in Genesis 39.

## OPEN 10 — Exact mechanism of prison keeper's trust

The LORD gives Joseph favour in the keeper's sight; the keeper delegates broadly. The chapter does not narrate the same explicit `saw that the LORD was with him` observation sequence used for Potiphar.

## OPEN 11 — Identity of Genesis 39 garment relative to Genesis 37 coat

Both are garments associated with Joseph, but Genesis 39 does not say this is the coat of many colours from Genesis 37.

Do not collapse them into one physical object.
